State legal gates — must pass before any recovery service

Tennessee: For Tennessee state-held unclaimed property, the Treasury says a locator must hold a Tennessee private-investigator license; the Treasury also says it does not itself regulate who can be a locator. A written locator contract must be submitted to Treasury for review and may not exceed 10% of the recovered value or $50, whichever is greater. The claimant is paid directly and the locator collects any approved fee separately. Do not assume these state-treasury locator rules govern court-held tax-sale surplus; check the court-surplus statute and obtain Tennessee legal advice separately. Official Tennessee Treasury locator guidance ↗
South Carolina: S.C. Code §30-2-50 prohibits knowingly obtaining or using personal information from state/local government records for commercial solicitation directed to people in South Carolina. Do not use county claimant lists to build a cold-call, mail or email marketing campaign. Under S.C. Code §12-51-130, after applying outstanding municipal tax liens, the remaining overage belongs to the owner of record immediately before the end of the redemption period. It is payable 90 days after execution of the tax deed unless another claimant institutes a judicial action during that period; unclaimed/unassigned overages escheat after five years from the public auction tax-sale date. Verify the deed execution date, any court action and county-held balance. SC Legislature §30-2-50 ↗ · SC Legislature §12-51-130 ↗
Tennessee tax-sale court surplus: Tennessee Code §67-5-2702 provides a court-motion process and priority rules among tax entities, lienholders and qualifying taxpayers/heirs. Davidson County's instructions state a $75 filing fee, require a signed motion and supporting documents, and require service on parties at least 30 days before the hearing. Under §67-5-2702, proceeds are prioritized first to tax entities, then qualifying lienholders, then the taxpayer or qualifying heirs; only remaining funds go to unclaimed property. This is a claimant/counsel court process, not a finder entitlement. A $75 fee makes this case not viable under the user's $0 budget unless the claimant or another lawful party pays it. Statute ↗ · Davidson County filing information ↗
South Carolina reported unclaimed property: S.C. Code §27-18-360 makes agreements within 24 months after property is paid/delivered to the state unenforceable and caps covered locator compensation at 15%. This is a different regime from county tax-sale overages; do not mix the rules. SC Code §27-18-360 ↗

Mandatory checks before any paid assistance

  • Confirm exact fund type: county tax-sale overage, foreclosure surplus, or state-held unclaimed property.
  • Confirm current amount, custodian, case status, filing deadline and whether funds were paid or transferred.
  • Identify legal priority and claimant category from law and official records; do not infer heirship from a surname.
  • Check licensing, private-investigator, attorney, claims-agent, solicitation, fee-cap and contract requirements in the relevant state.
  • Obtain claimant-initiated contact or documented authorization through a lawful channel. No cold solicitation from public-record personal information.
  • Have a qualified attorney review any compensation contract before it is offered or signed.
  • Do not advance a case if the claimant must pay fees the user cannot cover; show all fees and who must pay them.

Required case workflow

  1. Discover: find a published public balance or official surplus list.
  2. Verify custodian: confirm directly against the current official register, docket or agency record.
  3. Check case status: search motions, hearings, orders, liens, redemptions and disbursement entries. “No result” is not proof no claim exists.
  4. Determine legal claimant: document the applicable statutory priority and the evidence required. Estates/heirs require special scrutiny.
  5. Compliance gate: verify whether the proposed service requires a PI, attorney or other license and whether outreach/fee agreements are permitted.
  6. Authorization: only proceed when the rightful claimant independently authorizes lawful assistance. The bot never initiates claimant outreach.
  7. Claim: claimant or their attorney signs and files the claim/motion. Bot may organize a checklist and draft neutral inquiry text, not legal pleadings presented as counsel.
  8. Disbursement: record official payment confirmation to the claimant. Any separate fee is handled only under the valid agreement and applicable law.
$0 rule: The bot does not pay filing fees, buy records, retain counsel, purchase leads or advance expenses. Any case requiring money before recovery is marked “not viable under current $0 budget” unless a lawful third party has agreed in writing to cover it.

Payment is due 90 days after execution of the tax deed unless another claimant institutes a judicial action during that period. What to verify

  1. Correct court, case number and parcel.
  2. Motion/petition to claim surplus, objections and service.
  3. Hearing date, continuance or appeal/stay.
  4. Signed order deciding priority and entitlement.
  5. Disbursement/payment entry and custodian confirmation of remaining balance.
